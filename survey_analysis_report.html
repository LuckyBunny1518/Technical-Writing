Publication Date October 2026 (Expanded Cohort)

1. Executive Summary & Abstract

The integration of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI)—including Large Language Models (LLMs) such as ChatGPT, GitHub Copilot, and Gemini—has radically altered how university undergraduates retrieve, process, and consolidate academic knowledge. While LLMs offer unprecedented speed, syntax generation, and rapid troubleshooting, their habitual use introduces severe concerns regarding cognitive offloading: the systematic delegation of internal memory consolidation and intellectual synthesis to external digital engines.

This empirical report presents quantitative and qualitative findings derived from an expanded survey of N = 110 undergraduates across diverse academic disciplines at Woxsen University (Computer Science, AI & Data Science, Business Administration, Legal Studies, Pure Sciences, and Architecture).

Key Empirical Findings & Statistical Inferences (N = 110)

  1. High AI Saturation: 86.4% (n = 95) of respondents utilize Generative AI tools on a daily basis for university assignments, programming tasks, and academic research.
  2. Pronounced Retention Decay: 84.5% (n = 93) report a self-perceived decline in long-term memory retention since adopting AI (12.7% reporting severe degradation; 0.0% reporting improvement).
  3. The Daily Usage Threshold Effect: Among daily AI users (n = 95), 97.9% report retention decay and 97.9% report a loss of problem-solving independence. In sharp contrast, moderate weekly users (n = 15) report 0.0% retention decline (χ² = 87.67, p < 0.00001).
  4. Bypassing the Productive Struggle: 84.5% acknowledge AI has made them "much less independent" when facing difficult problems. Preference for AI summaries over documentation (M = 3.98/5.0) correlates almost perfectly with rapid forgetting (r = 0.984, p < 0.001).
  5. Disciplinary Vulnerability: Engineering/CS students suffer the steepest retention decay (95.5% in technical cohort), driven by "Syntax Amnesia" from automated code autocompletion and error diagnostics.

6. Theoretical Grounding & Academic Inferences

The empirical dataset strongly substantiates four cornerstone psychological and pedagogical theories:

  1. The Google Effect on Steroids (Sparrow et al., 2011): While search engines conditioned users to remember where information is stored rather than the information itself, LLMs eliminate even the navigation and keyword synthesis phase. Natural language prompting provides pre-synthesized answers, eradicating retrieval cues.
  2. Cognitive Offloading & Transactive Memory (Risko & Gilbert, 2016): Students treat GenAI as an external cerebral prosthetic. By offloading both data discovery and synthesis, internal neural networks fail to consolidate conceptual schemas.
  3. Retention Decay in AI Users (Barcaui, 2026): The 84.5% retention decline mirrors Barcaui's controlled trial where university students using ChatGPT remembered significantly less material after 45 days. Bypassing the Generation Effect (Slamecka & Graf, 1978) impairs durable retention.
  4. The Illusion of Explanatory Depth (Fisher et al., 2015): Fluent, authoritative AI prose induces an artificial sense of mastery. Students mistake the AI's fluent articulation for their own internal cognitive competence.

7. Actionable Pedagogical Recommendations

Curricular Interventions for Technical Writing & Engineering Programs

  1. Reintroduce "Cognitive Friction" into Assignments: Technical writing coursework must require students to trace and document their discovery path across primary sources rather than submitting synthesized summaries.
  2. Closed-Environment Conceptual Recall Assessments: To counteract "Syntax Amnesia," laboratory assessments and coding exams should enforce disconnected, unassisted environments without autocomplete or Copilot.
  3. Scaffolded Socratic Prompting: Students should be trained to utilize GenAI as an interactive Socratic interlocutor (requesting hints, logical audits, or adversarial critiques) rather than a turnkey solution generator.
  4. The "Read-Close-Synthesize" Protocol: Require students to review documentation or AI explanations, close the digital terminal, and write synthesis drafts from internal memory to activate neural encoding.
